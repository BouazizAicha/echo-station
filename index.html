The sky is the
radio dial.

Point your phone at the real Moon, Mars, Ceres or Pioneer 10. The signal rises as you get closer, and the arrow tells you where to turn. No sensors? Drag the sky map instead.

HTTPS required for phone sensors. Open ?debug on the phone during tests.

01

Real positions

Moon and Mars come from astronomy-engine. Ceres and Pioneer 10 come from NASA/JPL Horizons, generated by tools/horizons_to_json.py.

02

Aim anywhere

Phone sensors use a 3D-vector smoothing and a one-time calibration on the Moon. On a computer, drag the 90° sky map with a mouse, a finger or the arrow keys.

03

Lock the signal

Strength is 1 − distance / 20°. Hold at least 70% for 1.5 s — about 6° from the target — and the robot is found.

How the test harness works

This page contains only role A (sky, antenna, guide, map, counter, light-time). The story screen belongs to Dev B: the lock panel is a placeholder where B plugs the real story.

  • Antenna: phone held upright, back camera toward the target. North ≈ 0°, east ≈ 90°, camera up 45° ≈ alt 45°.
  • Calibration: aim at the Moon, tap Calibrate on the Moon. Phone compasses are typically off by 10–20°.
  • Sky map: ←→↑↓ pan, Shift + arrows go faster, Home recentres on the Moon.
