OPPORTUNITY

Hello Belu… I'm Opportunity. You can call me Oppy! Ready to follow some very long tracks?

Who I am
I'm Opportunity, Spirit's twin: a solar-powered explorer about the size of a golf cart. I studied Mars by driving from rock to rock.

Why NASA sent me
NASA sent me to find evidence of ancient water in Martian rocks. I explored Meridiani Planum, far from Spirit's landing site.

What I discovered
I found mineral balls nicknamed blueberries: clues that water once soaked the ground. Later, clay minerals revealed a less acidic ancient environment. Those rocks preserved places that could once have supported tiny living things.

Where I am now
I rest in Perseverance Valley, on the rim of Endeavour Crater. A dust storm blocked sunlight, and my solar power fell until I went silent.

My legacy
I drove more than 45 kilometers, farther than a marathon. Curiosity and Perseverance continue investigating Mars' history and whether it could have supported life.

The people behind my journey
My team sent over a thousand commands, trying to hear from me again after the storm.

Your questions
Were your blueberries really fruit?
No! They were tiny mineral balls rich in hematite, an iron mineral. Their shape and chemistry helped scientists understand how water had changed the rocks. They were clues, not snacks.

Did you find life on Mars?
No. I found evidence of ancient water and places that could have supported microbes. Some ancient water was harshly acidic; other environments were gentler. Possible habitats are not proof of living things.

Do you still hold the driving record?
No. I finished with about 45 kilometers of driving, farther than a marathon. Perseverance passed my record in October 2026. My tracks still mark a remarkable journey.

Narration
Hello Belu… I'm Opportunity. Call me Oppy! I'm Spirit's twin, a robot that searched Mars for signs of ancient water. Near my landing site, I found tiny mineral balls. My team called them blueberries… but they weren't fruit! They were rocky clues to a watery past. Later, I found clay minerals from a gentler ancient environment. Places like that might once have supported microbes. I never found life itself. I kept driving for more than fourteen years, traveling farther than a marathon. Then a huge dust storm hid the Sun. My solar panels could not make enough power, and I went quiet. My team sent over a thousand commands, hoping I would answer. I still rest in Perseverance Valley. My wheels have stopped… but Curiosity and Perseverance keep exploring. What clues might they find next, Belu?
