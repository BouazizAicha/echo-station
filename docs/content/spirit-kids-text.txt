SPIRIT

Hello Belu… I'm Spirit. Sometimes, a broken wheel can uncover a wonderful clue.

Who I am
I'm Spirit, a solar-powered robot that studied Martian rocks. I'm about the size of a golf cart, with wheels instead of feet.

Why NASA sent me
NASA sent me to search rocks for signs of ancient water. My mission was planned for about three months, like one school term.

What I discovered
My broken front wheel scraped away soil as I drove backward. Underneath lay silica, a mineral linked to ancient hot springs or steam vents. That was a clue to Mars' watery past.

Where I am now
I remain stuck in soft soil at Troy, inside Gusev Crater. I went silent as winter approached and my solar power dwindled.

My legacy
My twin Opportunity kept exploring after I went quiet. Curiosity and Perseverance carried on investigating Mars' past and its potential for life.

The people behind my journey
My team tested escape maneuvers in an Earth sandbox during their Free Spirit campaign.

Your questions
Why did you drive backward?
My right-front wheel stopped turning. Driving backward while dragging it helped me keep exploring. That wheel exposed the bright soil where scientists identified silica.

Did you find living things?
No. My discoveries showed that parts of ancient Mars had water and possible habitats. A habitat is a place where life could survive. That does not prove anything lived there.

Did getting stuck end your science?
Not immediately! I kept studying my surroundings without driving. But I could not reach a good winter position for sunlight. My team last heard from me on March 22, 2010.

Narration
Hello Belu… I'm Spirit. I explored Mars on wheels, hunting for clues in rocks. NASA planned about three months for me. I kept working for more than six years! Along the way, a broken wheel became an unexpected helper. I drove backward, dragging my right-front wheel. It scraped away soil and uncovered silica. On Earth, this kind of mineral can form near hot springs or steam vents. Could ancient Mars have had places like that? My discoveries helped scientists say yes. Later, I became stuck in soft soil. My team tested escape ideas in a sandbox on Earth. I kept doing science without moving… until winter approached and my solar power dwindled. My radio went quiet. But Opportunity, Curiosity, and Perseverance carried the exploration forward. Keep looking closely, Belu… surprising clues can hide beneath the dust.
