PIONEER 10

Hello Belu… I'm Pioneer 10. My radio is quiet now, but my story can still reach you.

Who I am
I'm Pioneer 10, a robotic explorer with a large, dish-shaped antenna. I carry instruments instead of passengers.

Why NASA sent me
NASA sent me to test a safe route through the asteroid belt. My next task was to study Jupiter up close.

What I discovered
I crossed the belt safely and sent back close-up views of Jupiter. I measured powerful radiation: tiny, fast particles that can damage spacecraft. Those measurements helped engineers prepare later missions.

Where I am now
I travel silently beyond Neptune's orbit, heading roughly toward the star Aldebaran. Earth last heard my faint signal in 2003, before you were born, Belu.

My legacy
Pioneer 11 followed my trail, then the Voyager probes explored farther. My measurements helped engineers design Voyager and Galileo for Jupiter's harsh surroundings.

A message from Earth
Carl Sagan, Frank Drake, and Linda Salzman Sagan created my plaque: a picture-message from Earth.

Your questions
Why did you go silent?
My generators turned heat from radioactive material into electricity. Over time, they produced less power. Eventually, my radio signal became too weak for Earth to detect. That ended our conversations, but not my journey.

Are you still the most distant spacecraft?
No. Voyager 1 overtook me in 1998 and travels farther from the Sun than I do. I was a trailblazer, but other explorers kept going.

Will aliens find your message?
We don't know. I carry a metal plaque showing people and clues to our place in space. It was made for anyone who might someday find me. It is a greeting, not evidence that aliens exist.

Narration
Hello Belu… I'm Pioneer 10. My radio is quiet now, but my story can still reach you. NASA sent me through the asteroid belt to study Jupiter. Nobody knew how dangerous that crossing might be. I made it through… then became the first spacecraft to fly past Jupiter. I sent close-up pictures home and measured particles that could damage spacecraft. Engineers used my findings to prepare other explorers. Pioneer 11 followed, then the Voyagers traveled farther. As the years passed, my generators produced less electricity. My last faint signal reached Earth in 2003. But I kept traveling. I now coast far beyond the planets, carrying a metal plaque. Its pictures show people and our place in space. A small greeting from Earth… on a journey still unfolding.
